# Week 1 to 4 concept checklist

C1 is one sixth of the Stage 1 mark and the difference between a 5 and a 3 is whether concepts
are *applied* or merely *named*. This file lists every concept the brief requires, and what the
artefact and the evidence file already give you to apply it with. The argument is yours; the
material is here.

Tick the box only once the concept appears in the 1,000 words doing actual work.

---

## Week 1: value creation

- [ ] **Value creation and capture.** Created: the customer stops spending a Sunday cooking and
      still hits a number. Captured: a margin per tray. Note the honest version, that the value
      created is time plus adherence, and adherence is the harder one to prove.
- [ ] **Startup versus small business.** Searching, not executing. The price, the coverage share
      and the tray sizes are all still hypotheses; the artefact exists to test them.
- [ ] **Problem, customer, current alternative.** The current alternatives are named and priced in
      `data/research.json`: My Muscle Chef, Dineamic, Muscle Meals Direct, Youfoodz, plus cooking
      it yourself and plus doing nothing. Do not omit the last two.
- [ ] **New combinations.** What is actually recombined here is not the food. It is
      calorie targeting (a tracking app behaviour) applied to a fulfilment business, plus four
      portion multipliers on one recipe base so one cook serves four calorie bands.
- [ ] **Risk versus uncertainty.** Risk: spoilage, delivery failure, ingredient cost. All
      estimable. Uncertainty: whether anyone pays a premium over My Muscle Chef for lower
      carbohydrate. Not estimable, only testable, and it is the biggest one.
- [ ] **Opportunity cost and economic cost.** Calculation 7. Needs your hourly rate and the hours
      this would take, which is the one input no research can supply.
- [ ] **Innovation rent.** Calculation 6. Your price is known ($13.90 to $22.90) and the
      incumbent's is known ($10.95 to $15.95). Only the two cost figures are missing. Say how long
      the gap survives: recipe reformulation is not defensible, so honestly, not long.
- [ ] **Factors of production.** Labour (cooking, packing), capital (commercial kitchen hire,
      trays, sealer), software (this artefact, hosting), and the courier. Note that the software
      is nearly free and the kitchen is not, which is the opposite of most ventures on this unit.
- [ ] **Four requirements of a viable business.** Real problem: partly evidenced. Preferred
      solution: not yet evidenced. Working unit economics: blocked on `cost_to_serve`. Value
      capture: a margin per tray. Be honest that two of four are unevidenced.

## Week 2: markets, demand and structure

- [ ] **Market definition formula.** "We are entering the market for **hitting a daily macro target
      without cooking** among **people who train three or more times a week in Greater Melbourne**
      in the context of **weekday meals**, currently served by **My Muscle Chef, Dineamic, Muscle
      Meals Direct, cooking in bulk on Sunday, and doing nothing**."
- [ ] **From vague to testable.** That definition tells you exactly who to interview: someone at a
      Melbourne gym who trains three or more times a week and currently either meal preps or buys
      ready meals. It excludes general healthy eating.
- [ ] **Demand schedule.** Calculation 2. Instrumented, needs traffic and a price variant.
- [ ] **Existing supply.** Four named competitors, plus the informal supply of cooking it yourself,
      plus doing nothing. The informal providers matter here because they are free.
- [ ] **Excess demand or excess supply.** With four funded competitors in the ready meal category
      and one gym specific player, argue this is a well served market overall with a thin
      low carbohydrate niche, rather than an underserved one. That is the harder and more honest read.
- [ ] **Startup capacity share.** Calculation 5. Blocked on `kitchen_capacity`.
- [ ] **Demand shifters, the "why now".** Open. Three candidates listed in `data/research.json`,
      each needing a dated source before it is used.
- [ ] **Type of good and income elasticity.** A convenience premium on a necessity. Argue it is a
      normal good with income elasticity above one for this segment, then note the implication:
      it is the first thing cut in a downturn.
- [ ] **Substitutes, complements, network effects.** Closest substitute is My Muscle Chef at
      roughly $3 a meal less. Complement you depend on: gyms, and the tracking apps people already
      use. **No network effects.** Say that plainly; a meal is not more valuable because others buy it.
- [ ] **Market structure.** Monopolistic competition: many sellers, differentiated product, low
      barriers to entry. That classification is what caps your pricing power, and it should drive
      the pricing section rather than sit as a label.

## Week 3: willingness to pay, elasticity and pricing

- [ ] **Willingness to pay.** Anchor it on the competitor prices in calculation 4 and on the cost
      of the current alternative (a Sunday of shopping and cooking, valued at the customer's own
      hourly rate).
- [ ] **The buying rule P ≤ WTP.** Apply it at each of your four tray prices.
- [ ] **Total revenue.** Calculation 2, TR = P × Q at every candidate price.
- [ ] **Own price elasticity, midpoint.** Calculation 3, computed automatically once data exists.
- [ ] **Elasticity along the demand curve.** State where on the curve you intend to price and why.
- [ ] **Elasticity and revenue.** Use your own elasticity figure to say what a price rise does.
- [ ] **Cross price elasticity.** What happens to your demand if My Muscle Chef cuts to $9.95.
      Given they ran a $7.95 special on the access date, this is not hypothetical.
- [ ] **Pricing power.** Assess against differentiation (low carbohydrate, real but copyable),
      switching costs (near zero, no lock in anywhere in this category), substitutes (many), and
      trust (none yet, pre revenue). Conclusion should be that pricing power is weak.
- [ ] **Price architecture.** Already built: four tray sizes, a volume discount at 15, 30 and 45
      meals, and a free delivery threshold at $120. Justify each.
- [ ] **Price discrimination and tiering.** The four tray sizes are second degree price
      discrimination by quantity: a customer on a cut self selects Regular, one building self
      selects XL. Show why each tier prefers its own.

## Week 4: first cost check only

- [ ] **Cost to serve one customer.** Calculation 8. Blocked on `cost_to_serve`.
- [ ] **Does the price cover it.** State the gap and whether it looks workable.
